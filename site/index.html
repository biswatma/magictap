How it works

Your MacBook already has the sensor

Apple silicon MacBooks from the 2021 MacBook Pro and 2022 MacBook Air redesigns contain a Bosch BMI282 six-axis motion sensor, sitting behind the Sensor Processing Unit. It is there for the lid-angle mechanism. MagicTap listens to it.

01 — READ

800 samples a second

The sensor streams calibrated acceleration at 800 Hz. A knock on the case shows up as a sharp transient riding on top of gravity, which is tracked separately and subtracted away.

02 — DETECT

Find the strike

A tap is a peak in that residual, above a threshold and outside a 0.12 s refractory window. Below 0.12 s the ringdown of one hard tap starts counting as a second — a floor measured from real recordings, not guessed.

03 — CLASSIFY

Left or right

Struck off-centre the chassis pivots, so lateral and vertical motion couple with a sign that depends on which side was hit. The correlation between those axes separates the two cleanly — 40 out of 40 on labelled recordings.

Why a correlation? Because it is scale-invariant. Multiply the whole waveform by any constant and the measure does not move — so how hard you tap cannot leak into which side it thinks you tapped. Measured separation on real data: d′ = 5.71.
Actions

51 built-in actions

Bind one to a double tap, and optionally different ones to a single tap on the left and on the right. Five take a value you type in — a keystroke, an app, a URL, a Shortcut name, a shell command.

Accuracy

The hard part is not detecting taps

It is refusing everything else. A laptop chassis is struck constantly — by typing, by being picked up, by being dragged across a bed. Each of those had to be measured and ruled out separately.

Typing is not a tap

Keystrokes are impacts on the same case, and two inside the double-tap window look exactly like a double tap. MagicTap asks the window server how long since real input and ignores taps within 0.35 s of a keystroke — timing only, never content.

A tilt is not a tap

Pick the Mac up and the gravity baseline no longer matches, which once made every sample read as a tap forever. The baseline now resyncs, and a tap must actually settle to count — a strike rings and dies, a new posture does not.

A drag is not a tap

Sliding it across a bed makes friction bumps that rise and settle just like taps. What separates them is isolation: a deliberate tap happens on a still machine. Taps arriving mid-motion are dropped.

Install

Four steps

Download and move to Applications

Open the DMG and drag MagicTap across. Launch it from Applications, not from the disk image — macOS ties permissions to a specific copy of an app.

Open it the first time with right-click → Open

MagicTap is not notarised by Apple, so a plain double-click is refused. Right-click the app, choose Open, confirm. Once is enough.

Grant the permissions it asks for

Screen Recording for screenshots, Accessibility for keystrokes and window management. The setup window walks through both and tells you which your chosen actions actually need. Quit and reopen afterwards.

Tap twice

Knock the case beside the trackpad. Hands off the keyboard first — taps during typing are ignored on purpose.

Prefer to build it yourself? git clone the repo and run ./build-app.sh. There is also a command-line version for recording, replaying and tuning detection.
FAQ

Questions

Which MacBooks does it work on?

Apple silicon MacBooks from the 2021 redesign onward: MacBook Pro with M1 Pro or M1 Max and every model since, and MacBook Air from M2 onward. Those are the machines that carry the motion sensor MagicTap reads.

Intel Macs, the M1 MacBook Air and the M1 13-inch MacBook Pro do not have it and cannot be supported. MagicTap tells you at launch rather than silently doing nothing. macOS 14 or later is required.

Does it work on a desktop Mac?

No. The sensor exists to measure the angle of a laptop lid, so a Mac mini, Studio or iMac has no equivalent.

Will it fire while I type?

No. Keystrokes are genuine impacts on the same chassis — this was the first bug found in real use. MagicTap asks the window server how long it has been since real keyboard or trackpad input and ignores taps within 0.35 s of it.

That check reads timing only. It never sees which keys you pressed, and it needs no Accessibility or Input Monitoring permission to do it.

Will it fire when I pick up or move my Mac?

It shouldn't. Two separate guards handle it. A sustained change in orientation — tilting, lap use, being carried — resyncs the gravity baseline instead of reading as an endless stream of taps, and a tap has to settle to count at all.

Separately, a tap that arrives while the machine is already in motion is dropped, which is what stops dragging it across a bed from firing. Both are switchable in Settings if you want to see the raw behaviour.

Can I use it on my lap, or in bed?

Yes. Once your posture settles, the baseline follows the new orientation and taps register against it. What is ignored is the moment of movement itself — while you are actively shifting or sliding the machine.

Why does macOS say the app is from an unidentified developer?

Because it is not notarised by Apple. Notarisation needs a paid Apple Developer account. Right-click the app and choose Open the first time and macOS will remember.

The same limitation means MagicTap cannot go on the Mac App Store: reading this sensor requires private system interfaces that App Store review does not allow.

Why does it need Screen Recording and Accessibility?

Screen Recording is required by macOS for anything that captures the screen, so the three screenshot actions need it. It is used only at the moment you trigger one.

Accessibility is required to synthesise keystrokes and to move other apps' windows, so roughly half the catalogue needs it. Screenshots, volume, lock, sleep and connectivity do not.

MagicTap refuses to run an action whose permission is missing rather than trying and failing, and the settings screen flags any action you pick that needs one you have not granted.

Does it watch my screen or record what I type?

No. MagicTap reads one thing continuously: the accelerometer. The typing guard reads the time since the last input event and nothing about the event itself.

Screen Recording is exercised only in the instant you fire a screenshot action, and the image goes to your clipboard or your Desktop. The app makes no network requests at all — there is no analytics, no account and no update check.

How does it tell left from right?

When you strike the case off-centre, the whole chassis pivots. Lateral and vertical motion therefore couple together, in phase for one side and out of phase for the other. Correlating those two axes across the tap separates them.

On the machine it was developed against the two sides sat at +0.32 and −0.80, a separation of d′ = 5.71 with no overlap across 42 labelled taps. The boundary is specific to one chassis, and the repository includes the tool to re-derive it for another model from twenty taps a side.

How fast is it?

Around 50 ms from tap to action. The detector waits 40 ms after the peak to see the full shape of the strike before deciding, which is the floor — it cannot classify a tap it has not finished observing. Fast enough to feel immediate.

What about battery?

MagicTap listens to a sensor your Mac is already running for its lid-angle mechanism, holds no timers of its own and makes no network requests. It has not been put through formal battery benchmarking, so no percentage figure is claimed here.

Single taps as well as double?

Yes — a single tap on the left and on the right can each have their own action. They are unbound by default, because a single tap has to wait out the double-tap window to prove no second tap is coming, and leaving them off keeps stray knocks silent.

Are there actions that do not fully work?

Four worth stating plainly. Toggle Focus runs a Shortcut you name, because macOS exposes no public API for Focus modes. App switcher sends ⌘Tab but cannot step further, which needs the key held down. Current weather opens the Weather app rather than reporting inline. Bluetooth uses a private system interface and reports itself unavailable if a future macOS removes it.

It is not detecting my taps.

Take your hands off the keyboard and stop moving the machine, then tap the case rather than the trackpad — a firm knuckle tap beside it works best. The setup window has a live monitor that shows every tap it sees, including ones it chose to ignore and why.

If taps still do not register, lower the tap-strength slider in Settings. If they register but double taps do not, widen the double-tap window.
